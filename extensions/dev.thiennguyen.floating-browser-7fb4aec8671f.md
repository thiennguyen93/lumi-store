Floating Browser is a small browser that stays over every app while you work in them — docs beside your editor, a recipe over your notes, a video in a corner.

## What you get

- **Over every app.** It floats above the app you are in, on every desktop, and over apps in full screen. **⌃⌥⌘H** brings it up from any app and puts it away again, your tabs kept. It opens without taking the keyboard from the app you are in — click it to type.
- **Look something up as you work.** Copy a word, press New Tab, paste: the answer shows beside your work.
- **Tabs, as in Chrome.** Up to four, an address bar that also searches, and a new tab that opens on a search box and your own shortcuts. **Open in your browser** for anything more.
- **Phone or desktop, per site.** The window is narrow, so sites open in their phone layout — and YouTube in its desktop one, whose player answers the mouse. Switch a site to the other layout and it opens that way every time.
- **Anywhere, any size.** Drag it by its title bar, resize it by its edges.
- **Compact Mode.** One slim bar in place of the tabs and the toolbar, so the page gets the room. The icon at its left counts your tabs and lists them.
- **Out of the way, still playing.** **⌃⌥⌘B**, or −, turns it into a small pill in the corner of the screen, and the music plays on: the song's cover, its title over its artist, and a band that fills as it plays. Hover it for the time played and a button to pause or play; press or drag along its bottom edge to go to another place. Click the pill to bring the browser back, or **Lumi** at its left for your tabs. Drag it to another display, and the browser comes back there.
- **In the menu bar.** Its own icon lists your tabs, with their sites' icons and a speaker on the one playing, beside Compact Mode, Mute All, Close All Tabs and Quit. A tab's speaker mutes that tab.
- **History and Settings.** **⌘Y** and **⌘,**, each in a tab of its own. Choose Light, Dark or System; the cover on the pill as a Spinning Record, a circle or a square; and whether the pill takes the cover's colour.

## Private by design

The pages are drawn by Lumi, over https, and the extension never sees inside them — only each page's address and title. Sites remember you between openings, kept apart from your other browsers. Your tabs, your history and your shortcuts are kept on this Mac.

## Getting started

1. Install, and the Welcome tour shows the browser at work — and sets its two shortcuts.
2. Press **⌃⌥⌘H** in any app, and type an address or a search.
3. Press **⌃⌥⌘B** to tuck it into a pill, and again to bring it back.

Needs Lumi 1.67 or later. More about extensions at [lumikeys.app](https://lumikeys.app/docs/extensions).
